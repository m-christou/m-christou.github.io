Description
======

(Co-taught with Dr. William Peden)

The course explores philosophical questions in the social sciences. In Part 1, we
shall review key theories and topics in general philosophy of science. There will be
a special focus on the relevance of these topics to the social sciences, especially
sociology.
In Part 2, we shall transition to the philosophy of the social sciences.
An overarching set of questions in the course are about the authority of the social
sciences. Why should policymakers and the general public regard sociologists,
economists, political scientists etc. as having special expertise? Why should major
decisions in public policy be informed by the social sciences? To what extent can
the social sciences justifiably attain the authoritative status of the natural sciences?

**Semesters taught**: Summer Semester 2026

Topics
======

How do the Social Sciences Explain, Values in Science, Experimentation and Social Policy, Epistemic Injustice, Social Policy and Well Being
